title: The Last Train Home
category: historias-poesia

The last train out of Halden left at 11:40, and Mara had missed it by four minutes. She stood on the platform anyway, holding a paper cup of coffee that had gone cold, and watched the red light shrink into the dark.

"You can wait for the milk run," the ticket clerk had said. "It leaves at five."

So she waited. The station had one bench, a clock that ran slow, and a vending machine that hummed a single note. She counted the hum against her pulse and found they never quite agreed.

Around two, an old man sat down at the other end of the bench. He carried a cello case instead of a suitcase, and he held it the way people hold a sleeping child. Neither of them spoke. At some point he opened the case, tuned the strings by ear, and played one slow phrase, over and over, changing it a little each time.

Mara did not know the piece. She knew only that it was *unfinished*, the way a sentence is when someone is still deciding how it ends.

> Some things are only ever played on empty platforms.

When the five o'clock train arrived, he closed the case and nodded to her. **She nodded back.** It was, she thought later, the longest conversation she had that year.
